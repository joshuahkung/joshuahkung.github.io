## The Frontier and the Rolodex

*Week of August 4, 2026 · DREAM fellowship, Chilton lab*

With the demo made hard and the engine made general, this week was about the thing in the middle: how do you show a ranked field of people so the organizer can make the marginal call — and hand it back when even the agent can't make it?

I built the ranked frontier: candidates ordered not by raw skill but by *value* — skill combined with a reliability weight — so a slightly weaker but dependable player can outrank a stronger flake. The interesting machinery is at the cut. When the two candidates straddling the boundary are within ε of each other in value, the agent's own metric can't split them, and that's the signal to stop pretending. The cell surfaces a small skill-versus-reliability comparison for just those two, highlights which dimension favors whom, and — when skill and reliability point at different people and the value is even — frames it honestly as a toss-up: *the optimizer is indifferent; this one is yours.*

That "this one is yours" is the same escalation machinery from week 6 — the novelty gate. An unforced pick at a genuine tie becomes a *tracked tie-break rule*, the same proposed→confirming→autonomous loop I use everywhere else. The organizer isn't just clicking a name; they're minting a policy about how they weigh dependability against talent at the margin, and the agent will propose it back next time.

I also built the timeline this week — a swim-lane view of who was asked, who replied, who went quiet — because the frontier is a snapshot and coordination is a process. A "yes" three days ago from someone who's now gone silent is not the same as a fresh "yes."

**What I learned.** The interface has two jobs at the cut, and the second is the harder one: make the marginal call tractable, *and know when to hand it back*. A tool that always renders a confident recommendation is lying whenever the underlying values are tied — and those tied cases are exactly the ones where the human's judgment is the actual content. Refusing to fake a recommendation, and converting the human's real call into a durable rule, is worth more than a cleaner-looking auto-pick.
