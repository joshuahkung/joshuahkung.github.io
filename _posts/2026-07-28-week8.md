## Making It Situation-Agnostic

*Week of July 28, 2026 · DREAM fellowship, Chilton lab*

The claim in my week 6 post was that I'd built a *domain-agnostic engine* with a thin per-task adapter. This week I tried to actually cash that claim, and it exposed how much basketball had leaked into the supposedly general core.

The test I set myself was blunt: can I relabel the whole thing to a completely different coordination problem *without touching the engine code* — only by swapping the adapter? I tried two. The office-hours case: a professor staffing a TA session, where "on court" becomes "staffed," the "ball" becomes "a room," "the gym" becomes a time slot. And a dinner. If the board still reads correctly with only a JSON adapter changed, the engine genuinely doesn't know what a "ball" is.

The first pass failed in instructive ways. The renderer had `🏀`, "the gym," "on court," and "Best N" baked directly into it. The reliability weights — how much a flake history should discount a score — were constants sitting in the display layer. The count of players needed was hardcoded to three. None of that is general; all of it had to move into the adapter or be read from state. By the end, the identical rendering code produced "Staffed · 6 of 6 · 🚪 Room" for the office-hours relabel, which is the proof I wanted.

Alongside that I adopted a rule that did a lot of cleanup work: **every line is either STATE or ACTION — nothing in between, and no standalone statistics.** "Replies: 2 in, 2 out" is a statistic, not a decision; it tells you nothing about what to do, so it went. Role and position labels, at that point, were noise too — they didn't change whether you were ready or what you'd do — so they went as well. (I'd bring positions back later, but as a real constraint, not decoration.)

**What I learned.** Generality is a *testable property*, not an architectural aspiration you assert in a README. The test is a relabel: if swapping a small adapter turns a basketball board into an office-hours board and nothing reads wrong, the seam is real. Every place the relabel broke was a place the engine secretly knew too much. The discipline of forcing every line to be state or action is the same discipline — anything domain-specific that survived was usually a statistic I hadn't justified.
